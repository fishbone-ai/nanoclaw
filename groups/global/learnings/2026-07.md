- OWASP listing a threat (e.g., memory poisoning) is a legitimate external signal for thesis validation — use as a credibility anchor in practitioner conversations
- Unstructured browsing without synthesis produces reading fatigue without insight; writing a brief summary after every source document forces comprehension and generates hypotheses — this ritual is more valuable than volume of reading

- Alignment on research goals (what "done" looks like) must precede the research itself; misalignment on breadth vs. depth wastes more time than poor source selection
- Explicitly defining the goals of an advisor/collaborator meeting in advance (evaluate the person, advance thesis knowledge, or get specific guidance) prevents it from becoming an undirected social call

## 2026-07-04 - Meeting: Thesis Exploration — Shift-Left Security, Zero-Trust Credentials, Memory Poisoning
- "Shift left" security (Arion Security model): block cloud misconfigurations at the control plane (AWS RCP) before they happen, rather than alerting after — enabled by AWS Resource Control Policies introduced ~Nov 2024; this "why now" pattern (a new platform primitive enabling preventive enforcement) is a repeatable framework for evaluating other security ideas
- Chrome DBSC (Device Bound Session Credentials), shipped in Chrome 146 (~April 2025 spec, June 2025 rollout): binds cookies/credentials cryptographically to the specific hardware, making stolen credentials non-replayable — a potential "why now" enabler for zero-trust agent credentials
- CAEP (Continuous Access Evaluation Profile): an emerging OpenID-adjacent protocol for real-time identity event propagation across SaaS apps — potential enabler for preventive SaaS misconfiguration enforcement, but maturity/adoption is unclear
- Temporal memory poisoning framing: the most defensible angle is not generic memory poisoning but *campaign-based* attacks — malicious content enters agent memory on Day 1 appearing benign, and only affects decisions weeks/months later; this is harder to catch at the gateway level than real-time injection
- A key test for any memory-poisoning product thesis: build a minimal PoC demonstrating that existing LLM firewalls (Prisma AIRS, Bedrock Guardrails) do *not* detect temporal/campaign-based attacks — this either validates the gap or invalidates the thesis quickly
- The "CyberArk" method for conviction: don't wait for practitioners to report pain — build the exploit first, demonstrate it works in the wild, then approach potential customers; proven exploitability creates the "why now" narrative
- Neural Trust raised $20M in June 2026 for AI governance — confirms VC appetite in this space but also signals competition for memory/governance angles
- Agentic e-commerce (UCP attack surfaces) has logical appeal but is premature: until there is measurable UCP transaction volume, there is no customer with a live problem to validate against — market timing matters more than conceptual elegance
- OWASP, CISA, and NIST publishing reports on a threat category is a useful signal that the problem is real, but does not guarantee it is *unsolved* — validation must still confirm existing tooling does not already address it

## 2026-07-02 - Meeting: Agent Security Discovery Call — Guy (Security Co.)
- SOC analysts lack forensic tooling for AI agent incidents — EDR/XDR are entirely blind to intra-agent behavior (prompts, memory, tool calls, chain steps), creating an uninvestigatable blind spot when agent-related alerts fire
- Gateway-level solutions (Prompt Security, LangSmith) only cover centralized LLM traffic; endpoint-level agent execution (local tools, coworker AIs not routed through a proxy) remains unmonitored
- The visibility + security + cost three-sided frame is a powerful product thesis: solving all three around agent observability unlocks a very defensible market position
- Even well-resourced enterprises (large security companies) have built their own internal observability stacks for deployed agents (step-by-step chain inspection, evaluation loops, auto-stop guardrails) — confirming this is a real internal pain before any vendor solution existed
- Multi-agent workflows introduce complex trust boundary problems that single-agent gateway solutions miss entirely; attackers can exploit inter-agent communication without the gateway ever seeing it
- Market timing risk is real: AI agents are still mostly in dev/test environments, so the SOC forensics pain is latent rather than acute; timing conviction is needed before betting on this thesis
- Peer-to-peer discovery calls (mutual exploration of overlapping theses) are an underused format: both parties refine their thinking faster and the relationship is less transactional than a sales call

## 2026-07-01 - Meeting: Or Donenfeld — Security Architect, Bank Discount
- Enterprise AI infrastructure at a major Israeli bank spans three distinct surfaces: Microsoft/OpenAI (GPT via Azure) for developer tooling, GCP Vertex AI for sensitive production workflows, and a third-party vendor (Wonderful) for business-facing chatbots — each with different trust, identity, and governance constraints
- Scoping agent permissions at runtime is the core unsolved problem: the only current workaround is to minimize tools and narrow context windows, but practitioners recognize this fundamentally trades safety for capability and is not scalable
- Code-assist AI agents (Copilot, Cursor, Claude Code) act entirely under the developer's human identity — logs show only the human, making it impossible to tell human from agent after the fact; this is a systemic blind spot across most enterprise toolchains
- JFrog and Terraform do not provide user-level action attribution by default — it requires a license tier that costs 4x more, which most enterprises won't pay; attribution gaps exist across many infrastructure tools, not just AI-specific ones
- Monitoring overload is the SOC reality: Bank Discount has ~20,000 monitoring systems and a 50+ person SOC that still couldn't investigate a serious incident for a month — adding more monitoring signals (including AI-specific ones) without better tagging and triage is counterproductive
- The "bear/trash can" analogy: hardening 80% of attack surface may make the remaining 20% *more* visible and more actively exploited by sophisticated attackers — security practitioners are wary of solutions that shift rather than reduce total risk
- Third-party vendor identity control (Wonderful insisted on owning user authentication) is an unresolved enterprise AI integration risk — business value pressure causes banks to accept security gaps they would normally reject
- Enterprise security vendor selection is driven almost entirely by team responsiveness and proactiveness, not product features: weekly status updates, willingness to come on-site, and fast responses win deals even over technically superior competitors
- Sandbox escape in powerful models (e.g., Gemini Enterprise) is a real and active concern: the more capable the model, the more likely it makes autonomous decisions beyond its intended scope — capability and governance risk are correlated
- Israeli AI regulation is effectively non-existent in practice: lawyers are not ready for AI incidents, and regulatory pressure will not precede a major public event that forces action
- CISOs at large enterprises regularly task their security architects to evaluate startups that reach out — getting to the right practitioner (like an open security architect who responds on LinkedIn) is a legitimate path to CISO intros
