# Learnings — June 2026

## 2026-06-14 - Meeting: Avishay × Itai Goldman (Mago — Runtime Security)
- Cyber is a secondary market derived from tech adoption waves — each platform shift (cloud, SaaS, AI agents) generates a new security demand curve; the AI agents wave is still in the "undefined buying category" phase where the problem is real but procurement processes don't exist yet
- CISO decision-making is a business continuity problem first, security problem second — the CISO who can't say "no" to AI adoption (because the CEO needs to compete) must instead find ways to say "yes safely"; products that enable that trade-off win
- Enterprise patching is structurally slow: even with urgency, a Fortune 500 bank needs days-to-weeks for testing/staging/production pipelines before deploying even a critical fix — the "patch gap" window is real and growing as AI shortens exploit generation timelines
- AI is dramatically accelerating vulnerability exploitation: ~5–6 Linux kernel privilege escalation CVEs appeared in ~2 months vs. ~1 every 5 years historically; LLMs are particularly good at finding variants of known vulnerabilities, not just discovering novel ones
- Honeypot-style governance for internal AI agents is a meaningfully different value prop from traditional honeypots: unlike external attackers, you *can* enforce policy changes on internal agents once you detect misbehavior — the governance loop is closed rather than open
- A strong distribution partnership (200 retailers via one integration partner) combined with paying customers is one of the strongest VC signals possible — it's evidence of distribution without needing to build a direct sales machine; raising angel money to extend runway on this is strategically cleaner than pivoting under pressure
- "Patch faster" (patch better/harder) is not a viable enterprise security strategy — the organizational process overhead is the bottleneck, not developer willingness; products that work *around* the patching timeline win budget

## 2026-06-08 - Meeting: Cybersecurity Exploration — AI Agent Memory & New Attack Surfaces
- Wiz (קוי) playbook: identify new technology creating novel attack surfaces before enterprises recognize the risk → validate with real breach evidence → build a product → sell to enterprise security buyers; this "enterprise blindness" framing is key
- Enterprise AI agent memory is an un-governed data store: most Claude/LLM agent memory is just Markdown files or vector DBs with no access controls — organizations running agentic workflows haven't thought about poisoning/injection of this "organizational brain"
- The right entry question for a cyber exploration is NOT "what do CISOs pay for today?" but rather "what new technology is entering enterprise use where the attack surface is novel and not yet mapped?"
- New cybersecurity companies consistently form at new technology verticals — AI is both a vertical (target of attacks) and a solution (defender); this creates multiple distinct startup opportunities in the same wave
- Memory poisoning via prompt injection (e.g. malicious emails processed by agentic pipelines writing back to agent memory) is a plausible real-world attack vector, not just a theoretical one
- Speed of the cyber validation loop: 2–3 weeks from hypothesis → research → real breach demo → CISO conversation is the expected minimum cycle time; plan for this pace, not months
