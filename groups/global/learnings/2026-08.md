## 2026-08-04 - Meeting: CISO Discovery — Andres Porras (Ria Money Transfer)

- Internal fraud (employees stealing client PINs/funds) is a larger daily concern than external hackers for money-transfer companies -- deception tech ICP extends beyond external threat actors
- Honeypot/deception tech has near-zero adoption despite high perceived value -- CISOs understand the concept immediately but have never implemented it
- AI governance tooling is a confirmed 2026 budget priority for mid-size financial services security teams -- spend availability is real
- CISOs are building GRC tools in-house using AI coding agents, but maintenance ownership is the breaking point -- the challenge isn't building, it's sustaining
- Existing AI governance tools (as envisioned by practitioners) focus on data access/policy enforcement -- they do NOT detect behavioral drift or goal-seeking deviation by agents
- AI-led attack frequency has visibly increased in the last 6 months; practitioners attribute it to both geopolitics and AI lowering the barrier to attack

## 2026-08-03 - Meeting: Dima / Semrush

- High-maturity CISOs (Semrush-level) self-build ephemeral agent containment systems -- they are builders, not buyers; Fishbone ICP is 1–2 maturity tiers below
- For experienced CISOs, early detection is not the bottleneck (75%+ confidence in ~5min is achievable); real constraints are human decision throughput and production not built for machine-speed response
- AI-led attacks are same threats at machine speed/volume -- not a new class; attacker disorganization is the only reason defenses haven't been overwhelmed yet
- Behavioral whitelisting (complete fingerprint of "normal" system behavior) is the path to autonomous machine-speed response without breaking prod
- Automating before fully understanding a process just speeds up the broken parts -- process documentation is a prerequisite for safe automation
- Security teams at mature orgs are becoming internal innovation hubs for agentic architecture, with internal non-security teams pulling their solutions
- Agentic containment requires BOTH environment isolation AND intent constraints in the prompt -- isolation alone fails if instructions don't prohibit escape (Hugging Face precedent)
- Technology lock-in is self-reinforcing: orgs commit until their own "next improvement cycle" -- stickiness is a function of change tolerance, not product superiority
